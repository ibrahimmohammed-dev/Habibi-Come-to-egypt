◆ About Luxor

The Ancient City of Thebes

Luxor, known in ancient times as Thebes, was the capital of Egypt during the New Kingdom period (1550–1070 BC). It served as the political, religious, and cultural heart of the most powerful empire in the ancient world.

Today, Luxor is home to the world's greatest concentration of ancient monuments — from massive temple complexes on the east bank to royal tombs carved deep into the limestone cliffs of the west bank. It is often called the world's greatest open-air museum.

◆ East Bank

The Great Temples

Built to honor the gods, these temples have stood for over 3,000 years

🏛️

Karnak Temple Complex

The largest ancient religious site in the world, covering over 200 acres. Built over 2,000 years by successive pharaohs, it contains temples, chapels, pylons, and the famous Avenue of Sphinxes. The Great Hypostyle Hall, with its 134 massive columns, is one of the most breathtaking spaces in ancient architecture.

🌙

Luxor Temple

Standing at the heart of the city, Luxor Temple was built primarily by Amenhotep III and completed by Ramses II. Unlike Karnak, it was dedicated to the rejuvenation of kingship. The temple is famous for its Avenue of Sphinxes, massive entrance pylons, and two towering obelisks — one of which now stands in Place de la Concorde in Paris.

✨

Avenue of Sphinxes

A 3-kilometer processional road lined with over 1,000 sphinx statues, connecting Karnak Temple to Luxor Temple. Recently restored and reopened, this ancient road was used for religious festivals and royal processions. Walking its length today feels like stepping directly into ancient Egypt.

◆ West Bank

Valley of the Kings

The royal necropolis of the New Kingdom pharaohs

⚰️

Valley of the Kings

For over 500 years, from the 16th to the 11th century BC, this valley served as the burial site for Egypt's most powerful pharaohs, including Tutankhamun, Ramses II, and Seti I. Over 63 tombs have been discovered, decorated with elaborate scenes from the Book of the Dead and other religious texts guiding the pharaoh through the afterlife.

👸

Valley of the Queens

Located south of the Valley of the Kings, this valley contains the tombs of queens, princes, and nobles of the New Kingdom. The most famous tomb here belongs to Queen Nefertari, wife of Ramses II, and is considered one of the most beautiful tombs in all of Egypt, with its vivid and remarkably well-preserved painted walls.

🗿

Colossi of Memnon

Two massive stone statues, each 18 meters tall, are all that remain of what was once the largest temple in Egypt — the mortuary temple of Amenhotep III. For centuries, the northern statue emitted a mysterious sound at dawn, attracting visitors from across the ancient world who believed it was the voice of the hero Memnon greeting his mother Eos, the goddess of dawn.

◆ Plan Your Visit

How to Visit Luxor

Everything you need to know before your trip

✈️

Getting There

  • Luxor International Airport has direct flights from Cairo
  • Trains run from Cairo and Aswan daily
  • Nile cruises depart from Aswan to Luxor
🌤️

Best Time to Visit

  • October to April — best weather for sightseeing
  • Avoid July and August — extreme heat
  • Sunrise visits to the Valley of the Kings recommended
💡

Useful Tips

  • Hire a local guide for the best experience
  • Photography is restricted in some tombs
  • Start early to avoid afternoon heat and crowds
